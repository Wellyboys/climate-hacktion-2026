# ReGround

> **Know where your soil is going before you dig.**

ReGround helps Auckland construction projects **reuse surplus excavated soil and rock locally** instead of sending suitable material straight to disposal.

Our idea is simple:

**Plan the destination before excavation → match with a nearby project → verify the material → reuse it locally.**

---

## The Problem

Construction and earthworks projects generate large amounts of excavated soil and rock.

In Auckland, around **2 million tonnes of soil are sent to disposal facilities each year** based on the Auckland Council material used in our research.

The issue is not only that surplus material exists.

The bigger problem is that construction teams may not know:

- where the material can go,
- who needs it,
- whether it meets the receiver's requirements,
- or whether the timing works.

This can lead to:

- unnecessary disposal,
- long-distance truck transport,
- stockpiling,
- additional handling,
- and higher project costs.

At the same time, another construction project may be purchasing similar material elsewhere.

# Our Solution

## ReGround connects surplus material with local demand.

```text
Construction Site
Surplus soil / rock
        ↓
     ReGround
        ↓
Material + Quantity + Timing + Location
        ↓
 Nearby Project
 needing suitable material
        ↓
 Verify Material
        ↓
      REUSE
```

Instead of deciding what to do with soil after excavation begins, ReGround helps construction teams **arrange the reuse pathway earlier**.

---

## How It Works

### 1. Forecast

Before excavation, the supplier provides:

- expected material type,
- estimated quantity,
- location,
- excavation dates,
- and available supporting reports.

### 2. Match

ReGround looks for nearby projects that need compatible material.

Matching considers:

- material requirements,
- quantity,
- timing,
- location,
- transport distance,
- and supporting evidence.

### 3. Verify

When excavation starts, the actual material can be checked against the agreed requirements.

Our proposed physical verification system can support checks such as:

- particle size,
- mass,
- moisture,
- and visual consistency.

Each batch is linked to a digital material record.

### 4. Decide

Each batch can receive one of three outcomes:

| Result | Action |
|---|---|
| **PASS** | Deliver to the matched project |
| **HOLD** | Further testing or review required |
| **FAIL** | Find an alternative destination |

Professional geotechnical or environmental testing may still be required depending on the project.

# What Makes ReGround Different?

Existing services already help with individual parts of the process, including:

- material exchanges,
- geotechnical testing,
- laboratories,
- soil processing,
- screening,
- and transport.

ReGround's proposed role is to **connect these steps into one source-to-receiver workflow**.

### ReGround focuses on:

**Planning + Matching + Verification + Routing**

The main idea is not simply to find someone who wants soil.

It is to make sure the **right material reaches the right project at the right time with the right supporting information**.

# Auckland Focus

We are initially focusing on **Auckland, New Zealand**.

Auckland construction sites can generate different excavated materials depending on location and geology, including:

- clay,
- silty clay,
- silt,
- sand,
- gravel,
- volcanic material,
- weathered rock,
- and basalt.

Because the material is not always the same, ReGround uses **material-specific matching** rather than treating all excavated soil and rock as one product.

# Environmental Impact

One major benefit of local reuse is reducing transport distance.

Our current model compares a longer disposal route with a shorter local reuse route.

Under our illustrative 2035 scenario:

- **222,666 tonnes/year** are routed to local reuse
- approximately **1,870 tonnes CO₂e/year** of transport emissions are avoided
- modelled transport emissions are around **8.7% lower**

These are **scenario results**, not measured Auckland-wide savings.

The actual impact would depend on:

- transport distance,
- adoption,
- material type,
- receiver demand,
- and project location.
